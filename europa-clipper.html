What Is Europa Clipper?

Europa Clipper is NASA's flagship mission to conduct a detailed scientific investigation of Jupiter's moon Europa — one of the most promising places in the solar system to search for conditions suitable for life. Launched on October 14, 2024, aboard a SpaceX Falcon Heavy rocket, it is the largest spacecraft NASA has ever built for a planetary science mission.

Europa has long fascinated scientists because beneath its frozen ice shell lies a global saltwater ocean containing more liquid water than all of Earth's oceans combined. Tidal forces from Jupiter's immense gravity flex the moon's interior, generating heat that keeps this vast ocean liquid despite Europa's distance from the Sun. Where there is liquid water, heat, and chemical nutrients — the basic ingredients for life as we know it may exist.

Rather than orbiting Europa directly (which would expose it to lethal radiation from Jupiter's magnetosphere), Clipper will orbit Jupiter and execute 49 targeted flybys of Europa over four years, building up a comprehensive picture of the moon's ice shell, ocean, and potential for habitability.

9 Science Instruments — Eyes, Ears & Senses at Europa

📡

REASON

Radar for Europa Assessment and Sounding: Ocean to Near-surface

Ice-penetrating radar that will transmit radio waves through Europa's ice shell and detect reflections from the base of the ice and the ocean beneath. Can probe up to 30 km deep. The primary instrument for measuring ice shell thickness and locating liquid water pockets within the ice.

🌈

MISE

Mapping Imaging Spectrometer for Europa

Infrared spectrometer that maps the composition of Europa's surface in 864 wavelength channels simultaneously. Will identify salts, organics, acids, and other chemicals — especially in the reddish-brown regions along the lineae that may mark where ocean material has surfaced through the ice.

⚗️

MASPEX

MAss Spectrometer for Planetary EXploration

Mass spectrometer designed to analyse the thin gas and plasma surrounding Europa. Will detect and characterise organic molecules and other chemical compounds in the exosphere, and is sensitive enough to sniff plumes of ocean material if they exist — the closest we can get to tasting the ocean from a flyby.

🌬️

SUDA

SUrface Dust Analyser

Dust mass spectrometer that captures and analyses individual dust particles ejected from Europa's surface by micrometeorite impacts. Each tiny grain can carry chemical fingerprints of the ice and the ocean below. Designed to detect biosignatures in material that has been blasted off the surface.

🔆

Europa-UVS

Europa Ultraviolet Spectrograph

Ultraviolet spectrograph that will search for plumes erupting from Europa's surface — jets of water vapour, like those seen at Saturn's moon Enceladus. Also maps auroral emissions produced where Jupiter's magnetic field interacts with Europa's thin oxygen atmosphere, which helps constrain the ocean's conductivity.

📷

EIS

Europa Imaging System

Wide-angle and narrow-angle cameras that will photograph Europa's surface in visible light at resolutions down to 0.5 m per pixel during close flybys. Will map the entire surface at 50 m/pixel resolution and identify sites of geological activity, chaos terrain, and any fresh material erupted from below.

🧲

ECM & ICEMAG

Magnetometers

Two magnetometers that measure Jupiter's magnetic field perturbations near Europa. The ocean, if it is salty and electrically conductive, induces its own magnetic signal — these instruments will detect and characterise that signal, providing direct evidence of a liquid ocean and constraining its depth, thickness, and salinity.

🌡️

E-THEMIS

Europa Thermal Emission Imaging System

Thermal infrared camera that maps the heat radiating from Europa's surface. Warmer regions may indicate thin ice, subsurface liquid water pockets, or recent geological activity. Will help identify the most geologically active — and potentially most habitable — locations on Europa's surface.

📻

PIMS

Plasma Instrument for Magnetic Sounding

Plasma analyser that measures the charged particles surrounding Europa. Works in tandem with the magnetometers to separate Jupiter's magnetic field signal from the ocean's induced magnetic signal — essential for accurately characterising the ocean's properties from the complex electromagnetic environment of Jupiter's powerful magnetosphere.

Exploring Europa — Past & Present

Earlier Missions to Europa
1979

Voyager 1 & 2

NASA · USA

First close-up images of Europa during Jupiter flybys. Revealed a strikingly smooth, cracked surface unlike any other moon — suspiciously lacking the craters expected on an ancient icy body. The smooth surface hinted that something was resurfacing it from below.

1995–2003

Galileo Spacecraft

NASA · USA

Conducted 11 close flybys of Europa during its Jupiter orbital mission. Detected Europa's induced magnetic field — the first strong evidence for a subsurface liquid ocean. Captured detailed images of chaos terrain, lineae, and possible plume sites. Laid the scientific foundation for Clipper.

2012–2019

Hubble Plume Detections

NASA / ESA

Hubble Space Telescope observed possible water vapour plumes erupting from Europa's south polar region on multiple occasions — similar to the confirmed plumes at Saturn's moon Enceladus. The detections remain tentative but suggest ocean material may be venting directly into space.

Europa Clipper — Mission Milestones
Oct 2024

Launch

NASA · SpaceX Falcon Heavy

Europa Clipper launched from Kennedy Space Center on October 14, 2024. At approximately 6,000 kg fully fuelled, it is the largest planetary science spacecraft ever built by NASA. The solar arrays span 30.5 metres (100 feet) — needed to generate power so far from the Sun.

Dec 2025

Mars Gravity Assist

Flyby

Clipper uses Mars's gravity to bend its trajectory and accelerate toward the outer solar system — a fuel-free speed boost. The flyby also serves as an opportunity to calibrate all nine science instruments in a real-space environment before arriving at Jupiter.

Apr 2026

Earth Gravity Assist

Flyby

Clipper swings back past Earth for a second gravity assist, using our planet's pull to achieve the velocity needed to reach Jupiter. Earth-based tracking stations use the flyby to verify navigation precision before the spacecraft departs the inner solar system for good.

Apr 2030

Jupiter Orbit Insertion

Science Campaign Begins

After a 5.5-year journey, Clipper fires its main engine to brake into Jupiter orbit. It enters a highly elongated orbit that repeatedly brings it close to Europa while spending most of its time farther out — avoiding the most intense radiation belts that surround Jupiter.

2030–2034

~49 Europa Flybys

Primary Science Phase

Over four years, Clipper executes approximately 49 targeted flybys of Europa (final count may vary slightly with trajectory). Altitudes vary by pass — the lowest approach ~25 km, with many flybys at higher altitudes for broader mapping and spacecraft safety. Each flyby covers a different ground track, building global coverage while instruments collect data during the brief close-approach windows.

2034+

End of Mission

End-of-Mission Disposal

To comply with planetary protection requirements and prevent any possibility of contaminating Europa with Earth microbes, Clipper will conclude its mission with a controlled disposal into the Jupiter system. The specific target body has not been definitively fixed in public mission documents — the priority is to avoid Europa. Ganymede has been discussed as one option.

Could Europa's Ocean Support Life?

Europa is considered one of the top candidates for extraterrestrial life in the solar system — not intelligent life, but microbial life. Its subsurface ocean is estimated to be 60–150 km deep, making it the largest body of liquid water in the solar system. It has existed for billions of years, kept liquid by tidal heating from Jupiter's immense gravitational pull.

At the ocean floor, scientists expect conditions similar to Earth's deep-sea hydrothermal vents — where superheated water erupts through cracks in the rock, carrying chemical energy. On Earth, entire ecosystems of bacteria, tube worms, and crustaceans thrive at these vents without any sunlight. Europa's ocean floor may offer similar chemistry.

The three requirements for life as we know it are: liquid water, a source of energy, and the right chemistry (carbon, hydrogen, nitrogen, oxygen, phosphorus, sulphur). Europa may have all three — but habitability is not confirmed. Europa Clipper is not designed to detect life directly. Its goal is to assess whether the environment could support it: mapping the ocean's chemistry, ice thickness, and geological activity to determine if Europa is truly a plausible habitat.

🎒 Middle School Analogy

Europa is like a sealed thermos kept warm from the outside. The lid is a 20 km thick shell of ice, and inside is an ocean bigger than all of Earth's oceans put together — kept liquid not by the Sun, but by Jupiter squeezing the moon like a stress ball, generating heat through friction deep inside. Europa Clipper is peering through the thermos wall to see what's inside, without ever opening it.

Mind-Blowing Europa Facts

🌊

More Water Than All Earth's Oceans

Europa's subsurface ocean is estimated to contain two to three times more liquid water than all of Earth's oceans combined. Earth's oceans hold about 1.335 billion cubic kilometres of water. Europa's hold roughly 3 billion cubic kilometres — hidden beneath 20 km of ice, in total darkness, 630 million kilometres from the Sun.

🔥

Tidal Heating Keeps It Liquid

Europa should be frozen solid at its distance from the Sun. It isn't — because Jupiter's immense gravity flexes Europa's interior as it orbits, generating frictional heat deep inside the moon. This tidal heating is the same process that creates the spectacular volcanic eruptions on Io, Europa's neighbour. Jupiter is Europa's heater.

🧊

The Ice Shell Moves Like a Conveyor Belt

Europa's ice shell doesn't sit still. Warm ice from the bottom rises, cold ice from the top sinks — a slow churning cycle called ice convection. This may be continuously recycling ocean chemistry up to the surface. The criss-crossing lineae are actually giant fractures where the ice pulls apart and new material wells up from below — like Earth's mid-ocean ridges, but in ice.

🛸

~49 Flybys But Never Orbiting Europa

Europa Clipper will never orbit Europa directly. Jupiter's radiation belts are so intense that orbiting Europa would kill the spacecraft's electronics within months. Instead, it orbits Jupiter — spending most of its time away from the radiation — and makes approximately 49 fast, targeted passes. Each flyby lasts only hours. The lowest planned approaches skim as close as ~25 km above the ice, though most passes occur at higher altitudes for mapping and safety.
